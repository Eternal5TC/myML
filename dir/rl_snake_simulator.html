Reinforcement Learning (Q-Learning) Architecture

A step-by-step breakdown of how tabular Q-learning enables an AI agent to master the game of Snake.

1 State Representation ($S$)

Instead of using raw pixels or absolute board coordinates, we reduce the environment state to an 11-bit relative vector:

  • Danger Ahead, Left, Right (3 bits: binary 0 or 1)
  • Food Direction (4 bits: Up, Down, Left, Right relative)
  • Current Direction (4 bits: North, South, East, West)

This spatial abstraction reduces the total state space to just $2^3 \times 4 \times 4 = 128$ distinct states!

2 Action Space ($A$)

At every discrete tick, the snake agent must choose one of 3 relative actions:

Action 0 Turn Left
Action 1 Straight
Action 2 Turn Right

The Bellman Equation for Temporal Difference Learning

$$Q(s, a) \leftarrow Q(s, a) + \alpha \cdot \left[ R + \gamma \cdot \max_{a'} Q(s', a') - Q(s, a) \right]$$
α (Learning Rate): Controls how heavily new information overrides old estimates.
γ (Discount Factor): Weight placed on future rewards vs immediate gratification.
$R$ (Reward Signal): Environment feedback received after executing action $a$.
$\max Q(s',a')$: Maximum estimated value reachable from the new state $s'$.

Epsilon-Greedy Strategy ($\varepsilon$)

To balance Exploration (trying random moves to discover optimal paths) and Exploitation (choosing the move with highest Q-value), we use an Epsilon decay strategy:

with probability ε → Choose Random Action
with probability 1-ε → Choose argmax Q(s, a)

Reward Function Design

The agent learns purely by feedback derived from its actions:

  • +10 points: Eating food unit.
  • -10 points: Colliding with wall or self body.
  • -0.1 point: Every step penalty to discourage endless looping.
